## 01 被冒犯后的心理争论 / The Argument in My Head

Yesterday, on my drive home, a car cut in front of me out of nowhere. It scared me, and I hit the brakes hard.

And I thought about it the entire way home. I had an argument with him in my head. By myself, of course.

It wasn't the first time, either. A barista snapped at me once. Another day, a group chat moved right past something I said. On days like that, it's all I can think about.

And here's the funny part. That same week, someone in a meeting took credit for work I had stayed up nights to finish. And in that moment, I said nothing.

A few days later I came across a line from Camus. He's the writer behind The Stranger, but this one is from his notebooks. "One must put one's principles in great things; for the small ones, mercy is enough." At first I read it as advice to be the bigger person.

But years later he quoted that line again, in the preface to another book, and added something. In his own case, he admits, what he calls mercy is really closer to indifference. A maxim he made to patch a hole in his own nature.

So he isn't telling me to be nice about it. He's telling me to stop deciding who was wrong in the first place.

It's strange, once you notice it. The second I start building my case, that person hardens into a name. The guy who cut me off. After that, every time he comes to mind, that name comes with him, and I stop asking why he did it. Maybe he was late for something. Maybe his day had already fallen apart. By then, none of that gets through to me.

So lately I try not to give people that name, even in my own head. Not the guy who cut me off, not the rude barista, none of it. It sounds small, but it isn't. When I skip the name, the story about that person stops right there.

The drive home is a lot quieter now. And whatever headspace that frees up, I'm saving it for the next time someone takes credit for my work.

## 02 不再计较 / Stop Keeping Score

There's a neuroscience finding that changed how I think about being happy.

Your brain really has just one reliable way to feel good: making the people around you happy.

Not as a nice idea. As how the brain is actually wired.

The strange part is, we usually go the opposite direction.

We treat the people around us as competition. Someone to keep up with, or beat.

And when everyone feels like a rival, you stay a little on guard, all the time.

That constant guarding is exactly what wears your brain down.

You get more defensive, more tired, and quietly less happy.

So being warm to people isn't just kindness for their sake.

It might be the most practical thing you can do for yourself.

Stop keeping score, and your brain finally gets to relax.

So after this, I really hope you take a second and think: what's one small thing, on your end, you could actually do?

## 03 准备好是在行动之后 / Ready Comes After

So I had this thing finished. It just sat in my drafts for like three weeks.

It was totally done. And every day I'd open it and go, "Not yet. It's not ready yet."

And I really believed that. It felt responsible, you know?

Just needed a little more polish, a bit more time, the right moment.

So I kept putting it off.

But here's the thing I finally caught myself on.

I kept waiting to feel ready, and that feeling just never came. Not once.

And one day it kind of hit me why.

If I actually felt ready, that would mean I'd already done it.

Ready is the feeling you get after. It's not the thing at the start.

It's the thing waiting at the end.

So me, sitting there waiting to feel ready before I posted it?

That's like standing at the start of a race, waiting to feel like I'd already crossed the finish line.

It's never gonna happen. That's just not how it works.

Nobody has their ducks in a row and feels calm about it.

They take the plunge while they're still scared. They wing it.

It's half-baked, rough around the edges, they're second-guessing the whole time, totally in over their head.

And then somehow, halfway through the mess, they've become the person who could do it. They didn't even notice it happen.

I still don't love that. I kind of wish there was a version where you feel ready first.

There isn't. You just dive in, learn on the fly, and the ready part shows up way later. That's if it ever shows up at all.

Anyway. I finally posted the thing.

It wasn't ready. Neither was I.

## 04 见到最好的自己 / Meet the Best Version of You

There's a version of you you've never met. The best version.

Not a fantasy, just you, at your absolute peak.

For some people it's their body. For others it's a project, a race, an exam.

Whatever it is for you, you already know the goal.

It's the one you keep pushing to next month, next year, once things settle down.

And things never settle down.

Most people go their whole lives without meeting that person once.

That's the saddest part. Not failing. Never finding out.

So stop negotiating with yourself and go.

Not for a week. Not until it gets uncomfortable.

All the way, even if it takes years.

I did that. I put in the years, and I met the best version of myself.

And now, when life knocks me down, and it still does, I don't stay down long.

Because I can tell myself, I've done hard things before. I know exactly how far I can go.

That's how you bounce back. Not with motivation. With proof.

And that proof doesn't fade.

Nobody can take it from you, because you were there. You did it.

So do it once. All the way. Meet the best version of you.

After that, no hard day can convince you that you're not capable. You've already proved it.

And that person is a lot closer than you think.

## 06 午睡后的听力练习 / Listening After a Nap

After a hectic week, I took a half-hour nap this - afternoon. But when I woke up, I felt completely groggy. Studying or getting any real work done felt out of the picture, so I decided to give in to my laziness and crawled right back into bed.

Still, I couldn't fall back asleep. In the end, I just headed to the gym to work on my English, focusing on speaking and listening. At first, I was just playing the audio over and over, and then it hit me. I had a sudden epiphany: I’ve never truly dedicated myself—heart and soul—to anything. That’s why I always seem busy, yet achieve so little.

Right then, I decided to train my focus, starting small—with just a single 48-second English clip.

The first time through, my comprehension was hazy at best. On the second try, I felt like I was starting to grasp a few things. By the third round, my focus slipped again, and random thoughts kept creeping in. But around the tenth listen, something clicked. I slipped into a flow state. I could clearly catch every single word, and for those 48 seconds, my mind was entirely locked in—no wandering thoughts, just pure listening.

Looking back, that was definitely my biggest takeaway of the afternoon.
